standard synthetic priors generate datasets that are too well behaved compared to the messiness of real world tabular data. synthetically generated datasets have no missigness, smooth marginals and no spurious correlations. These prior design choices makes it difficult for TFMs to outperform standard ML models on messy datasets.

#### O'Prior
a "compositional realism prior" basically a more complicated prior generator that are built from 4 knobs stacked together
- hierarchical SCM meta generator: combines function families rather than just using one or two functions per graph. This maps the real world complexities into these synthetic datasets. Feature-target relationships are not consistent in real world datasets, they can be made up of linear to very complex relationships depending on the feature.
- modular realism engine. fancy way of saying it just adds messiness to the data. after the scm produces fake clean data, O'PRIOR roughs it up by deleting some values, adding noise to labels, stretching number distributions
- punishing shortcut thinking. often times while modeling the training data may exhibit feature relationships that may not map to the test data. idea is to build this sort of pattern into these synthetic datasests to mimic real world data
- O'PRIOR configures realism difficulty via three presets, P e {LOW, MILD, HARD}. essentially a training curriculum where the further you go into training the harder the tasks become. 

### Experiment
- 40,000 synthetic datasets per prior. each of size. T e [512, 1024] rows and d e [3,50] features. batch size = 4 tables. 1000 steps per epoch. 10 epochs of training.
- compared against baseline generators: TabPFN-v1, TabICL-v1 and TabICL-v2. evaluated on 52 classification datasets from TabArena and OpenML
- O'PRIOR ablations: 9 variants constructed. 

### Results
- knob 1 resulted in the biggest improvement i.e. mixing multiple types of relationships into the data rather than sticking with only one or two
- adding realism resulted in further improvements suggesting heterogenous marginals, more missingness and noise are more beneficial for learning. There is however a non trivial interaction between maximal realism and structural diversity. 
- shift module also yielded improvements.
- curriculum method didnt necessarily work. ramping up difficulty didnt necessarily improve the model over models where you immediately expose the model to harder tasks.
- datasets generated by O'PRIOR resemble real world data better than TabICL-v2
- for each layer of a given model they trained a classifier to validate whether the model possesses internal understanding. models trained on O'PRIOR show increasing probing accuracy across layers. TabICL-v2 on the other hand exhibits flat or marginal improvement which suggests that O'PROR induces more rich internal representations.

### Conclusion
- O'PRIOR improves transfer by combining diverse structural mechanisms coupled with realism combinations and shift aware stress. 
- mechanism diversity and observational realism affect downstream performance significantly

#### questions
- if the o'prior generator is able to train a model that produces more separable classes through each layers, this might suggest that the model is learning the true casual relationships between features and target. Might it be that therefore it might create casual graphs that more closely resemble the true causal graph compared to current SOTA frameworks generators?
